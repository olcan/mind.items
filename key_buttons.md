#key_buttons shows the keys that navigate items as buttons, on a device without a keyboard: a row at the page's lower right, beside the `#vault` item's sparkle. A press sends the key, and the app does with it what it does from a keyboard.
- **←** **↑** **↓** **→** move from the item the box names: to its previous sibling, its parent, its first child, its next sibling.
- the arrow lights are hints from the named item's tree (green where the tree has a move, dimmed where not; still clickable), not an exact replay of the keys — the app can still step through a grandparent's tags or to the first visible item from an empty box when an arrow is dimmed, Left and Right can be dimmed beside a relative tag whose item is gone while the key still puts that name in the box, and Down can stay lit while the named item is open for editing. With no single item named, all four are dimmed; other buttons never change.
- **+** is Shift+Enter: a new item, under the named item when the box names one (a chat's reply).
- the **arrow up to a bar** is Ctrl+Alt+Up: the page scrolls to its top. The **arrow down to a ring** is Ctrl+Alt+Down: back to the position that left, else to the named item.
- the row shows where the primary pointer is a finger (a phone, a tablet), and hides while the on-screen keyboard is up.
- while the keyboard is up, a short row just above it: the **arrows to a bar** are Shift+Tab and Tab, the editor's outdent and indent of the caret's line, and the **tick** is Shift+Enter: an item's editor saves, the box creates. The row is for the app's editors (an item's, and the box): the keyboard of any other field gets none.
- on such a device, **Shift+Space** in an editor is a Tab.
- on every device, a round **reload** button (at the page's lower left on a phone, across from the `#vault` item's sparkle; beside the sparkle on a device with a keyboard): one press reloads the page, and asks first when an edit is not saved or a save is not finished (on an app that cannot say, it asks every time). On a touch device (a coarse pointer), both corner buttons — reload and the `#vault` sparkle — match the row's 32 px size, centred where they stand with a mouse.
- on a device with a keyboard (where the main row is hidden), the row's buttons stay on the page in one strip at the lower right, in three groups: top and back, the four arrows, new item; then the reload button, then the `#vault` item's sparkle. The arrows carry the same tree hints; a button's tooltip names its key; while any item is open for editing, the four arrows and the new-item button fade and ignore presses, so an open edit is never closed or discarded, while top and back stay bright and scroll as Ctrl+Alt+Up and Ctrl+Alt+Down do; closing the last editor brings the arrows' hints back and the new-item button to full brightness.
- another item (such as `#todoer`) can hand action buttons for the named item to the bar: in a wide window they stand at the strip's left end a group's gap away, and in a narrow window or on a phone they move to a second row above the bar's left end (lined up with the reload button on a touch device), hidden while the on-screen keyboard is up; a pressed button stays lit until its action finishes.
- a dark frosted-glass background sits behind the buttons, rejecting presses that start on bare glass outside the buttons' boxes (except over an embedded frame such as a video, which takes such a press itself) while letting scrolling over it work as before: a full-width bottom strip while the main row shows on a phone; on a device with a keyboard, one rounded piece at the lower right behind the strip, the reload button and the `#vault` sparkle, which becomes a full-width strip in a narrow window (with a second-row piece above the left edge whenever handed-over buttons stand there). Slow or dropped taps and page stalls (naming overlapping long animation frames on Chromium 123+, or slow input events on browsers with Event Timing such as Safari 26.2+ and Firefox) are logged in the console. Stall lines carry only numbers, fixed browser event/callback/invoker words, and standard HTML or SVG element tags (falling back to `element`; never ids, classes, attributes, text, values, or raw invoker strings). A script's function name, file and place are never said.
